Operational Context & Motivation

Problem Statement: Bottlenecks in Hospital Outpatient Departments

Operational Pain Points
  • Static FCFS Paradigm: Conventional systems treat all patients equally; emergencies wait behind routine wellness checks.
  • Departmental Silos: OPD, Diagnostic Labs, Imaging, and Pharmacy maintain isolated, disconnected queues.
  • Cascading Surges: Delays in morning OPD cause massive sudden congestion at blood draw and X-Ray counters.
  • Patient Unpunctuality: No-shows and late arrivals cause doctor counter starvation and scheduling instability.
Clinical & Patient Impact
  • Compromised Triage: Unstable patients (acute chest pain, high pediatric fever) face hazardous waiting times.
  • Severe Dwell Time: Multi-department visits stretch to 3–5 hours due to repeated manual queuing.
  • Counter Idling vs Jamming: Specialist doctors sit idle waiting for delayed patients while adjacent rooms overflow.
  • Patient Anxiety & Chaos: Lack of wait transparency leads to agitated crowds at nurse stations and help desks.
Real-World Operational Failure
  • Step 1: OPD Arrival: Takes token, waits 50 mins in General Medicine.
  • Step 2: Lab Referral: Doctor orders ECG & Troponin test. Patient walks to Lab.
  • Step 3: Disconnected Re-Queue: Takes 2nd paper slip, waits in 30-person queue with routine fasting checks.
  • Step 4: Imaging Delay: Waits in 3rd separate queue at Radiology.
  • Step 5: The Breakdown: By the time results arrive, doctor shift has ended or initial queue is 20 people deep.
Project Goals & Innovations

Core Research Objectives & System Deliverables

1. Dynamic Triage-Aware Priority Engine
  • Formulate an intelligent multi-factor priority algorithm integrating Clinical Acuity (ESI 0–2).
  • Incorporate appointment window punctuality vs walk-in arrival weighting.
  • Introduce a logarithmic aging factor: ln(1 + t - t_checkin) to permanently eliminate infinite starvation of routine patients.
  • Age vulnerability weighting for pediatric (<5 yrs) and geriatric (>65 yrs) care.
2. Multi-Stage Clinical Workflow Orchestrator
  • Model patient clinical care trajectories as Directed Acyclic Graphs (DAG).
  • Unified master QR token across all departments (Consult → Lab → X-Ray → Pharmacy).
  • Automated background queue registration and pre-notification for downstream counters.
  • Preserve cumulative waiting tenure across inter-department handoffs.
3. Adaptive Wait-Time Forecasting & Public Kiosks
  • Calculate rolling EWMA of consultation durations over preceding 2-hour windows.
  • Provide confidence-bounded wait intervals: [We · (1 - α), We · (1 + β)].
  • Contactless self-service touch kiosk for rapid token generation.
  • Wall-mounted public TV displays with audio chime announcements.
4. Sub-Second Event Bus & Grounded AI Guidance
  • Socket.IO bidirectional event pipeline ensuring sub-100ms state dissemination.
  • Doctor room locking mechanism (Available → Calling → Occupied → Completed).
  • Grounded AI Assistant (Google Gemini LLM) answering token status and prep FAQs.
  • Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) with complete event-sourced audit logging.

Critical Need for Research

The Central Research Gap & The SmartQMS Solution

5 Fragmented Paradigms in Existing Literature
  • Single-Stage Queuing Island: Optimizes ER or OPD in isolation, ignoring downstream multi-department referrals.
  • Archaic FIFO Token Systems: Digital LED screens still use blind First-Come-First-Served order.
  • Passive Predictive Models: Forecast wait times without actively routing or reordering tokens.
  • Opaque AI Black-Boxes: Deep RL decisions cannot be audited or overridden by clinical doctors.
  • Disconnected Information Silos: Zero real-time sync between kiosks, doctor rooms, and hall displays.

“How can a unified healthcare queue platform dynamically orchestrate multi-department workflows, adaptively balance clinical urgency with waiting-time fairness, provide confidence-bounded wait estimates, and maintain sub-second multi-client synchronization across physical hospital counters?”

  • Dynamic Fair-Priority Scoring (DFPS): Triage + Punctuality + Aging.
  • Sub-Second Reactive Event Bus: Real-time Socket.IO broadcasts.
  • Explainable Architecture: Auditable rules with doctor overrides.

Mathematical Modeling

Mathematical Formulations: Dynamic Priority & Adaptive Wait

1. Dynamic Fair-Priority Scoring (DFPS)
P_i(t) = w_1·Acuity(i) + w_2·ApptPunctuality(i,t) +
         w_3·ln(1 + t - t_checkin) + w_4·AgeVulnerability(i)
  • Acuity(i) ∈ {0, 1, 2}: Clinical triage severity rating (0 = Routine, 1 = Urgent, 2 = Emergency).
  • ApptPunctuality(i, t): Bell-curve weight peaking if arrival is within the scheduled appointment window.
  • ln(1 + t - t_checkin): Logarithmic aging factor guaranteeing that waiting time steadily elevates any patient's rank, eliminating starvation.
  • AgeVulnerability(i): Priority weight for pediatric (<5 yrs) and geriatric (>65 yrs) patients.
2. Adaptive Wait-Time Forecasting (EWMA)
W_e = (N_ahead · μ_service) / C_active
Confidence Interval = [ W_e · (1 - α), W_e · (1 + β) ]
  • N_ahead: Count of uncalled tokens in current queue with higher priority rank.
  • μ_service (EWMA): Rolling Exponentially Weighted Moving Average of actual consultation durations over the last 2 hours:
    μ(t) = λ·S_current + (1-λ)·μ(t-1) [λ = 0.2]
  • C_active: Number of currently active/available doctor rooms in the department.
  • [α, β] Margins: Dynamically scaled by service time standard deviation.
Clinical Process Engineering

Multi-Stage Workflow Orchestration: Chained Patient Journey

STAGE 1 Check-In Appointment QR or Walk-in Kiosk; token issued (e.g., C-001).
STAGE 2 Consultation Doctor calls next; examines patient; prescribes blood & X-ray tests.
STAGE 3 Auto-Transfer Step 1 completion auto-registers patient into Lab queue (L-014).
STAGE 4 Diagnostics Sample collected/scanned; results recorded; auto-routes to next stage.
STAGE 5 Pharmacy Prescription dispensed; master workflow marked COMPLETED.
Key Value Proposition: Zero Manual Re-Ticketing

Unlike traditional hospitals where patients must pull a fresh paper slip at every physical desk, SmartQMS maintains a single cryptographic master token. Cumulative wait times in previous queues are preserved and rewarded in downstream priority calculations.

Verification & Validation

Experimental Evaluation Plan, Baselines & Benchmarks

4 Benchmark Baselines
  • Baseline 1: Random / Unregulated Walk-in: Physical lobby crowds outside rooms without centralized tokenization.
  • Baseline 2: Pure FCFS Electronic Token System: Standard electronic token dispenser operating strictly on First-Come-First-Served.
  • Baseline 3: Static Appointment Scheduling: Fixed-slot scheduling with no adaptive handling of consultation overruns or walk-in surges.
  • Baseline 4: Disconnected Multi-Queue Systems: Department-isolated queues requiring manual re-ticketing at each stage.
Quantitative Metrics & Hypotheses
  • Average Wait Time (AWT): Mean elapsed duration from check-in to consultation start across all departments.
  • Total Hospital Dwell Time (THDT): Cumulative time for multi-stage visits (Consult → Lab → Pharmacy).
  • Counter Idle Ratio (CIR): % of operational hours doctor chambers remain idle while patients wait.
  • Triage Response Latency (TRL): Time taken to serve Category-1 (Emergency) arrivals.
  • Starvation Index: Maximum wait time of routine walk-in tokens during peak hours.
Target Hypotheses: 30%–45% cut in THDT, >60% cut in TRL, zero starvation, sub-100ms sync.
